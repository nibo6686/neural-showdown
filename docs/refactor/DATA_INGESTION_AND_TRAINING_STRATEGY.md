# Proposed Data Ingestion and Training Strategy

**Date:** 2026-10-08  
**Status:** Proposed; architecture discussion only.  
**Deciders:** Project owner; acceptance pending.

## Context

The long-term goal is a recommendation-only Gen 9 Random Battle singles assistant that starts with human-action imitation and improves toward stronger battle win rate. The [product specification](../requirements/spec.md) remains authoritative. This proposal explains how human replay evidence could feed the refactored capture pipeline without leaking hidden opponent information or reconstructing incomplete owner information through guesses.

Complete-episode acceptance, FEATURE-001, dataset eligibility, training objectives, evaluation, and live inference still require separate decisions. `faithful_complete_episode:false` remains required. This document does not authorize collection, dataset generation, training, or live deployment.

## What `.inputlog` means

| Artifact | Plain-English meaning | Proposed use |
|---|---|---|
| Public replay `.log` | The spectator's account of what visibly happened | Check public events, ordering, reveals, and terminal outcome |
| Replay `.inputlog` | Instructions and initialization evidence used to reproduce the battle | Attempt to recreate teams, choices, and decision-time requests through Showdown |
| Reconstructed owned request | What one player was allowed to know and choose at that moment | Authoritative owner-side observation and legal action source, subject to validated reproduction |

Showdown's official API documentation describes a `.inputlog` endpoint for autogenerated-team formats and says replay JSON can contain both logs. Its reproduction documentation explains that saved input logs include a random seed and can be replayed using Showdown source. These are documented capabilities, not proof that every saved Gen 9 replay has a usable input log or works with this project's pinned engine. [API documentation](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown-client/blob/master/WEB-API.md), [reproduction documentation](https://pokemonshowdown.com/pages/rng).

The endpoint pattern is `https://replay.pokemonshowdown.com/<replay-id>.inputlog`. No replay was downloaded or reproduced for this proposal. Actual contents, availability, historical coverage, initialization semantics, and rejection behavior must be inspected in a separately approved feasibility task. An input log is not itself a saved player request or a ready-made training example.

## Decision drivers

- Human decisions provide a useful starting policy before model self-play is effective.
- Players know their own starting teams even when spectators never see those teams completely.
- Opponent knowledge must remain limited to evidence available at the exact decision cursor.
- Local Showdown should supply mechanics and requests rather than a second rules engine.
- Reproduction must be bound to compatible simulator, generator, and format identities.
- Training, evaluation, and eventual live inference should use the same accepted observation and action contracts.

## Options considered

| Option | Benefits | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Spectator logs alone | Human play; simple acquisition | Often lack owned moves, items, abilities, exact HP, PP, and unrevealed identities; observed actions do not establish the complete legal candidate set |
| Human input logs reconstructed locally | Human play plus potential recovery of authentic owner requests | Availability and historical version compatibility remain unproven; reconstruction and admission checks are required |
| Local heuristic battles | Controlled teams, requests, provenance, and outcomes | Demonstrations reflect heuristic weaknesses; useful for testing and initial comparisons |
| Model self-play | Scalable improvement and diverse trajectories | Requires a useful starting policy, robust opponents, compute, and safeguards against evaluation leakage |

## Proposed decision

Use **verified human replay reconstruction for initial imitation**, followed by **local simulator training against varied opponents and model checkpoints**. Keep spectator-only evidence in a separate category with explicitly limited permitted uses. Select this direction only after a feasibility study establishes usable input-log availability and reconstruction success.

This recommendation aligns with the existing replay-imitation goal; it does not accept a new data or model milestone.

## Proposed ingestion flow

1. **Acquire source evidence after authorization.** Preserve replay ID, URLs, retrieval time, supplied format/version metadata, public log, input log, and content hashes. Record missing artifacts and source access restrictions explicitly. Keep initialization, seeds, and full teams in reconstruction storage, outside model inputs.
2. **Select a compatible engine.** Bind simulator revision, random-team data, format/configuration, and resolver/environment provenance. A present-day seed may regenerate different teams under different historical data. Never assume all historical replays match `pokemon-showdown@0.11.10`.
3. **Reproduce forward.** Start from original initialization and feed recorded human choices into Showdown. Capture each player's owned requests and the eligible public prefix before consuming that player's action. Check action mapping against that request's legal candidates.
4. **Compare evidence.** Require agreement with recorded public gameplay events and terminal outcome under a documented normalization policy. Any excluded transport/timestamp records must be listed explicitly; gameplay differences cannot be normalized away. Verify original-request origin, lineage, privacy, and both-player continuity through accepted capture/publication checks.
5. **Admit or quarantine.** Publish authoritative imitation rows only when reproduction and the applicable contracts pass. Preserve structured failure reasons for missing input logs, incompatible versions, ambiguous identities, unsupported records, malformed choices, or incomplete evidence. Do not repair inputs using the chosen action or a later reveal.
6. **Build features separately.** A future accepted FEATURE-001 contract transforms the pre-action observation into model inputs. The human action is a target; future events and terminal outcomes are separate labels and cannot enter earlier inputs.

Public API availability does not establish collection permission, corpus representativeness, or training eligibility. Apply the existing [replay fixture policy](REPLAY_FIXTURES.md) and a separately approved collection policy.

## Own-team knowledge, opponent knowledge, and Illusion

Recovering a verified original owned team is legitimate because its owner knew it at battle start. Copying the final team state backward is unsafe: items, moves, PP, status, and identities can change during a battle. Forward reproduction must establish those changes at the correct boundary.

For unrevealed Zoroark, a successful reproduction should retain the actual roster identity in the owner's authorized view while preserving the opponent's publicly observed disguise. A public log alone may never resolve that identity. Such a log cannot be promoted to a complete owner-information example by assuming the disguised species was real.

The reconstruction process may know both teams and the PRNG seed. The policy receives only its own permitted information and causally available opponent evidence. It must never receive the seed, future choices, opposing requests, hidden sets, or an internal simulator snapshot. Outcomes may be training labels; they are not decision-time features. Live inference must work without a replay input log or access to the opponent's initialization.

## Proposed training and evaluation sequence

1. **Imitation baseline:** predict legal human actions using verified decision-time observations from both sides. Preserve available skill metadata and source provenance; determine filtering and weighting experimentally. Winning does not make every move a good move, and losing does not make every move bad.
2. **Controlled evaluation:** compare against random, damage-oriented, and more varied heuristic policies. Evaluate held-out human-action prediction and battle performance separately.
3. **Outcome-oriented improvement:** initialize from the imitation policy and train locally against a mixture of opponents, including earlier model checkpoints. Heuristics provide comparison points and diversity rather than a permanent performance ceiling. The learning algorithm, rewards, search integration, and promotion criteria remain undecided.
4. **Live recommendation testing:** after separate approval, consume owned requests and public events through the same feature boundary; begin with recommendation-only behavior and explicit unsupported-state handling.

Split by battle before producing rows so the two perspectives and reconstructed segments of one battle cannot cross train/evaluation boundaries. Deduplicate replay copies. Consider chronological and player-disjoint evaluation to measure distribution shift and memorization. Saved public replays may overrepresent interesting games or particular skill levels; measure that bias rather than assuming the corpus represents ordinary ladder play.

## Consequences and open questions

**Benefits:** human initialization, potentially complete owner information, authentic legal choices, and reuse of the simulator/capture boundary for human and local trajectories.

**Costs:** historical engine compatibility, reconstruction throughput, provenance storage, quarantine accounting, and additional dataset/privacy validation. Public-only records may remain unusable for authoritative action imitation even when they contain a terminal outcome.

Open questions include actual input-log availability, whether initialization contains teams or requires version-sensitive generation, supported historical revisions, request/choice reconstruction accuracy, corpus usage policy, throughput, rating-based selection, feature design, and reinforcement-learning objectives.
