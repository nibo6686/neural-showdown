> **Goal.** Turn reproduced human battles into a compact, leak-free training
> dataset with honest unknowns, ready for B2 to train M1.
>
> ### Part A — vocabulary
> Add `tools/engine/generate_vocab.cjs`, which reads the **pinned engine's**
> dex (species/formes, moves, items, abilities, types, natures not needed,
> status, weather, terrain, pseudo-weather, side conditions, volatiles that
> appear in public state). Write `trainer/src/neural/vocab_b8f23c99.json` with
> stable integer ids. Reserve ids for **PAD, UNKNOWN (not yet revealed) and
> ABSENT/NONE (known to be nothing)**: three different things, never merged.
> Pin it by SHA-256 like other engine assets. Rerunning the generator must give
> identical bytes.
>
> ### Part B — `feature-v1` contract and featurizer
> 1. Write `docs/contracts/FEATURE_V1.md` (short and precise): every tensor,
>    its shape and dtype, the source field in the v2 observation or own
>    request, normalization, and the known/unknown/absent encoding for each
>    field that can be unknown. Follow TRACK-B §3–4: 12 Pokémon tokens (own 6,
>    opponent 6 with **unrevealed placeholders**), 1 field token, and
>    variable-length **legal-action tokens** with a mask. Switch actions point
>    to the target's Pokémon token index.
> 2. Implement `trainer/src/neural/feature_v1.py`:
>    `featurize(observation, request) -> dict[str, np.ndarray]`. It's a pure
>    function of the owner's observation and own request only. **It must not
>    read** the K projection, the other owner's data, the protocol prefix
>    beyond what the v2 view already types, or anything from the future.
>    Opponent features use only public view fields. Own stats and moves come
>    from own view/request.
> 3. **Tests:**
>    - golden features for 3 committed fixture frames (pin the exact arrays);
>    - unknown vs absent vs zero cases (unrevealed opponent item = UNKNOWN; a
>      Knocked-Off item = ABSENT; 0 PP = known zero);
>    - a legal-action mask that matches the request exactly;
>    - **leakage invariance**: run the W9L invariance cases
>      (`tests/helpers/decision_frame_cases.ts`) through export and
>      featurization. The viewer's **feature tensors must be byte-identical**
>      before the reveal and differ after, in both directions;
>    - determinism (same input, same bytes).
>
> ### Part C — streaming dataset builder
> 1. Add `tools/dataset/build_b1.cjs` (or a Python driver calling the
>    exporter), run under the **R1 supervisor**. For each battle in a `--files`
>    list, born-reproduce it, export frames for both owners, and **stream them
>    to the Python featurizer** through stdin/stdout JSON lines or a worker pool.
>    Write compact shards only (`.npz`, about 10k examples each) to
>    `data/datasets/b1-s3000/` (git-ignored). **Don't store raw frames in
>    bulk**; keep at most a 50-frame sample for debugging.
> 2. Per example, store: features, legal-action mask, the human action index
>    (in the legal list), outcome label (win 1, loss 0, tie 0.5, from that
>    owner's perspective), ending kind (normal/forfeit/inactivity), rating,
>    battle ID, step and owner.
> 3. **Split** by battle ID, seeded 90/5/5, recorded in the manifest. Commit
>    `docs/datasets/b1-s3000.manifest.json`: commit, engine, frame schema,
>    feature version, vocabulary hash, the sample list's hash, split battle-ID
>    lists or their hash, counts per split, label definitions, and failures
>    (battles that didn't reproduce or export, with reasons).
> 4. **Run it** on `data/replays/corpus-2026-10-21/sample-3000-seed20261024.json`
>    in the **background under the supervisor** (normal priority, an immutable
>    worktree at your commit, the live view works). **Don't wait for it**:
>    validate the builder on the committed fixtures, commit, and report the
>    launch details. A short follow-up records counts when it finishes. Measure
>    a 50-battle pilot first and report the estimated total time.
